## 1. Script Kiddies

**Script kiddies** are individuals with less technical skills who use pre-written tools, scripts, and exploits created by others to launch attacks. They often do it for fun, curiosity, or to show off, rather than for a specific strategic goal.

> **Example:** A beginner using a downloaded "DDoS tool" or a YouTube tutorial to knock a website offline without understanding how the code actually works.

---

## 2. Cyber Criminals

**Cyber criminals** are individuals or organised groups motivated primarily by financial gain. They use their technical skills to steal data, commit fraud, extort money, or sell unauthorised access to other malicious actors.

> **Example:** Ransomware gangs encrypting a hospital's files and demanding cryptocurrency for the decryption key, or hackers stealing credit card details to sell on the dark web.

---

## 3. Insider Threats

**Insider threats** come from individuals who already have authorised access to an organisation's systems, such as current or former employees, contractors, or business partners. They can cause harm either maliciously (for revenge or money) or accidentally (through negligence).

> **Example:** A disgruntled employee downloading a company's proprietary source code to their personal drive before quitting, or an employee falling for a phishing scam that compromises the internal network.

---

## 4. Hacktivists

**Hacktivists** are attackers driven by ideological, political, or social motives. They use cyber attacks to promote a cause, protest against organisations, or leak sensitive information to expose perceived wrongdoing.

> **Example:** The group "Anonymous" defacing a government website to protest a new law, or leaking confidential corporate emails to expose unethical business practices.

---

## 5. Nation-State Actors

**Nation-state actors** (or Advanced Persistent Threats - APTs) are highly skilled, well-funded hacking groups sponsored by a government. They focus on espionage, stealing state secrets, disrupting critical infrastructure, or gathering intelligence for geopolitical advantage.

> **Example:** A state-sponsored group quietly breaching a country's power grid systems to plant backdoors for future use, or stealing intellectual property from foreign tech companies to boost their own national economy.
