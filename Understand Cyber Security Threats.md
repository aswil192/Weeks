## 1. Threats

A **threat** is any potential cause of an unwanted incident that could result in harm to a system or organization. It represents a danger that might exploit a vulnerability.

> **Example:** A malicious hacker, a piece of malware (like ransomware), or even a employee who intends to cause damage.

---

## 2. Vulnerabilities

A **vulnerability** is a weakness or flaw in a system's design, implementation, or operation that can be exploited by a threat. 

> **Example:** Using a weak password, having unpatched software, or leaving a database publicly accessible without authentication.

---

## 3. Risks

A **risk** is the potential for loss or damage when a threat exploits a vulnerability. It is often calculated as the combination of the likelihood of an event occurring and its impact.

> **Example:** If you have an unpatched server (vulnerability) and there are active hackers scanning the internet (threat), the risk is that your server gets compromised and data is stolen.

---

## 4. Exploits

An **exploit** is a specific piece of code, a tool, or a technique used to take advantage of a vulnerability to cause unintended or unanticipated behavior in a system.

> **Example:** A script that targets a known bug in a web application to gain unauthorized access to the server.

---

## 5. Attack Vectors

An **attack vector** is the specific path or method used by an attacker to gain access to a system or network to deliver a payload or malicious outcome.

> **Example:** Phishing emails (social engineering), compromised USB drives, or exploiting open network ports.
